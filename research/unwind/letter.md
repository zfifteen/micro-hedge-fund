Until it does, a lower list price remains a lower conversion rate on the installed base that the circular paper was sized against.

## Hub mark and the collateral

NVIDIA closed Monday at two hundred twenty-seven thirty-eight, about three point nine percent below the mid-May high of two hundred thirty-six fifty-four. The semiconductor index finished at twelve thousand four hundred thirty-three, still well below the June high of fourteen thousand six hundred fifty-five. The hub mark is the collateral for the next circular round. It is closer to the May high than it has been in two weeks. It is still intact enough that the danger level stays at three rather than moving to four.

## What would change the letter

The score moves to four if a named guarantee is restated, if a neocloud misses a payment, if another large cheap-tier cut arrives without a volume offset, or if NVIDIA falls more than fifteen percent from the May high while capital-spending guides stay up.

The score moves to five if a residual-value guarantee is drawn, if a hyperscaler cuts capital spending, or if a hub credit event prints.

The score moves to two only if mid-tier token prices stop falling for two quarters, neocloud rental holds, and the circular book stops growing.

Monday confirmed a firmer tape, an unchanged cheap-tier list-price stack, and a rental series dated the twentieth that still shows three thirty-seven on the public neocloud H100 cell. The conversion rate did not recover.

## The open question

The loop is a race between a falling conversion rate and a rising stock of promises. The question worth keeping open is whether tokens sold, at these prices, can service paper that was sized on a higher price.

This letter does not tell the cash account what to own.
