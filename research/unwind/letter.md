Official provider pages still show the July split. OpenAI lists GPT-5.6 Luna at twenty cents of input and one dollar twenty of output per million tokens. Terra sits at two dollars and twelve dollars. The flagship GPT-6 Astra lists at ten dollars and fifty dollars. Sol remains on promotional pricing of four dollars and twenty dollars through at least the twenty-first of November. Anthropic’s cards put Claude Opus 5 at five and twenty-five, Sonnet 5 at two and ten, Haiku 4.5 at one and five, and Fable 5.1 at ten and fifty. These are list prices, not realized revenue. Volume can still rescue the dollar total if buyers consume enough of the cheap tokens. No primary filing has yet shown that volume offset. Until it does, a lower list price remains a lower conversion rate on the installed base that the circular paper was sized against.

## Circular paper and spender cash

NVIDIA’s August filing still caps residual-value guarantees at the SB Energy Ohio site at one hundred five billion dollars, with OpenAI as the tenant. Amazon has closed fifty billion of OpenAI equity and added one hundred billion of AWS commercial commitment over eight years. That paper did not shrink on Wednesday. Announced is not funded is not drawn. Drawn is the print that would move this letter from three to five.

Spender free cash flow remains the live tension. Amazon’s trailing free cash flow is still negative seven point six billion. Alphabet printed negative five point nine billion in the second quarter. Meta printed seven hundred eighty million. Oracle printed negative five point four billion of free cash flow in the just-reported quarter, spent twenty-eight and a half billion of capital, and sold twenty billion of common stock through an at-the-market program. Full-year capital spending is still guided at ninety to ninety-five billion. July’s envelopes moved up at three of the four large spenders and none cut them. The Fed’s line that capital investment is robust is consistent with those envelopes. It does not convert those envelopes into incremental AI cash.

CoreWeave equity closed at eighty-three thirty-five on Wednesday, about forty-six percent below the fifty-two-week high of one hundred fifty-three twenty. Debt stock remains about thirty-five billion. Financing still closed. An equity print is not the same thing as a cheaper refinancing or a lower debt load. Wednesday’s three percent bounce is a mark. It is not a missed payment.

## Hub mark and the collateral

NVIDIA closed at two hundred thirteen ninety on Wednesday, about nine and six tenths percent below the mid-May high of two hundred thirty-six fifty-four. The semiconductor index finished at eleven thousand two hundred forty-six, six tenths of a percent above Tuesday and further from the June high of fourteen thousand six hundred fifty-five. The hub mark is the collateral for the next circular round. It is a little firmer than Tuesday. It is still intact enough that the danger level stays at three rather than moving to four.

## What would change the letter

The score moves to four if a named guarantee is restated, if a neocloud misses a payment, if another large cheap-tier cut arrives without a volume offset, or if NVIDIA falls more than fifteen percent from the May high while capital-spending guides stay up.

The score moves to five if a residual-value guarantee is drawn, if a hyperscaler cuts capital spending, or if a hub credit event prints.

The score moves to two only if mid-tier token prices stop falling for two quarters, neocloud rental holds, and the circular book stops growing.

Wednesday confirmed a modest hub bounce, a rim bounce, an unchanged list-price stack, a rental hour that ticked to three sixty-three, and a rate hike that praised capital investment without changing the conversion rate. The conversion rate did not recover.

## The open question

The loop is a race between a falling conversion rate and a rising stock of promises. The question worth keeping open is whether tokens sold, at these prices, can service paper that was sized on a higher price.

This letter does not tell the cash account what to own.
