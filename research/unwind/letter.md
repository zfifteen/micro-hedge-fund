# The Unwind Letter

**11 September 2026**
Danger level: **3, Stressed** — unchanged

This is a research letter, not a trading session and not a recommendation. Nothing in it is a short, a hedge, or an allocation.

---

Good morning.

Oracle reported its fiscal 2027 first quarter after the close on Thursday. Cloud infrastructure revenue more than doubled and remaining performance obligations climbed to six hundred sixty-four billion dollars. Capital spending ran hard enough to leave free cash flow negative by five billion dollars for the quarter. The print confirms demand and the size of the next round of paper. It does not reverse the July token-price cut or the August residual-value guarantee. NVIDIA closed Thursday at two hundred eighteen thirty-six, roughly seven and a half percent below the mid-May high. The danger level stays at three, Stressed.

The conversion-rate race is unchanged. Cheap-tier token list prices remain where they were set at the end of July. Neocloud GPU rental continues to clear well below the hyperscaler sticker. No guarantee has been drawn and no rim payment has been missed. The loop is still asking the installed silicon to service paper that was sized on a higher conversion rate while new paper continues to be written.

## Tokens and the conversion rate

Official provider pages still show the July split. OpenAI lists GPT-5.6 Luna at twenty cents of input and one dollar twenty of output per million tokens. Terra sits at two dollars and twelve dollars. The flagship GPT-6 Astra lists at ten dollars and fifty dollars. Sol remains on promotional pricing of four dollars and twenty dollars through at least the twenty-first of November. Anthropic’s cards put Claude Opus 5 at five and twenty-five, Sonnet 5 at two and ten. Google lists Gemini 3.1 Pro at two and twelve for prompts at or below two hundred thousand tokens, and Gemini 3.8 Flash at an introductory seventy-five cents and three dollars seventy-five through the end of the year. xAI lists Grok 4.6 at two and six below the long-context breakpoint.

These are list prices, not realized revenue. Volume can still rescue the dollar total if buyers consume enough of the cheap tokens. No primary filing has yet shown that volume offset. Until it does, a lower list price remains a lower conversion rate on the installed base that the circular paper was sized against.

## The price of an hour

GPU rental tells a similar story by venue. The CCIR series printed on the tenth of September put a guaranteed H100 at ten dollars fifty-three an hour on a hyperscaler, three dollars sixty-one at a neocloud, and three dollars four on the marketplace. H200 printed ten thirty, four forty, and four dollars. B200 printed fourteen twenty-four, six sixty-nine, and five eighty. An interruptible H100 at a neocloud still cleared near two fifteen.

A residual-value guarantee written against a 2025 rental deck is looking at a different number than the deck that is clearing today. Hyperscaler stickers staying high does not rescue a neocloud that financed at the low print. The first place this race becomes cash is not NVIDIA’s mark. It is a payment date at the rim.

## Circular paper and spender cash

NVIDIA’s August filing still caps residual-value guarantees at the SB Energy Ohio site at one hundred five billion dollars, with OpenAI as the tenant. Amazon has closed fifty billion of OpenAI equity and added one hundred billion of AWS commercial commitment over eight years. That paper did not shrink on Thursday. Announced is not funded is not drawn. Drawn is the print that would move this letter from three to five.

Spender free cash flow remains the live tension. Amazon’s trailing free cash flow is still negative seven point six billion. Alphabet printed negative five point nine billion in the second quarter. Meta printed seven hundred eighty million. Oracle printed negative five billion of free cash flow in the just-reported quarter and guided full-year capital spending high enough to keep the balance-sheet funding model in place. July’s capital-spending envelopes moved up at three of the four large spenders and none cut them. The loop is still paying for silicon out of the balance sheet and the bond market rather than out of incremental AI cash.

CoreWeave equity closed at eighty-nine twelve on the tenth of September, giving back more of the prior bounce and still well below the fifty-two-week high. Debt stock remains about thirty-five billion. Financing still closed. An equity print is not the same thing as a cheaper refinancing or a lower debt load.

## Hub mark and the collateral

NVIDIA closed at two hundred eighteen thirty-six on the tenth of September, about seven and a half percent below the mid-May high of two hundred thirty-six fifty-four. The mark is the collateral for the next circular round. It remains intact enough that the danger level stays at three rather than moving higher.

## What would change the letter

The score moves to four if a named guarantee is restated, if a neocloud misses a payment, if another large cheap-tier cut arrives without a volume offset, or if NVIDIA falls more than fifteen percent from the May high while capital-spending guides stay up.

The score moves to five if a residual-value guarantee is drawn, if a hyperscaler cuts capital spending, or if a hub credit event prints.

The score moves to two only if mid-tier token prices stop falling for two quarters, neocloud rental holds, and the circular book stops growing.

Oracle’s print confirmed the scale of demand and the continued reliance on balance-sheet and equity funding. It did not unwrite the July token cut or the August guarantee.

## The open question

The loop is a race between a falling conversion rate and a rising stock of promises. Thursday’s Oracle numbers raised the stock of promises. The question worth keeping open is whether tokens sold, at these prices, can service paper that was sized on a higher price.

This letter does not tell the cash account what to own.
