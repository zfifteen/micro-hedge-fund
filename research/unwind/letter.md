# The Unwind Letter

**5 September 2026**
Danger level: **3, Stressed** — unchanged

This is a research letter, not a trading session and not a recommendation. Nothing in it is a short, a hedge, or an allocation.

---

Good morning.

The finance loop that built this cycle is still running, and it is still running on two clocks that do not agree. One clock is the price of the thing the machines actually sell: tokens and GPU hours. The other clock is the paper that paid for the machines. Cheap-tier token prices were cut hard at the end of July. NVIDIA filed a $105 billion residual-value guarantee in the middle of August. Those two facts have not been walked back. Friday's tape made the second fact easier to live with. It did not make the first fact go away.

That is why the danger level stays at 3. It is not 4, because no guarantee has been drawn, no neocloud has missed a payment, and no hyperscaler has cut capex. It is not 2, because the conversion rate that turns a GPU into cash is weaker than the promises that funded the last build, and spender free cash flow is already the subject of the earnings call.

## What Friday changed

NVIDIA closed at $230.36 on Friday, about 2.6 percent below the mid-May high of $236.54. The Philadelphia semiconductor index closed at 11,735, up more than three percent on the day and still about twenty percent below the June high. CoreWeave stock bounced to $89.36. None of that is a credit event. It is a mark. The mark is the collateral that lets the next circular round get written, and the mark is intact. That is the bull case for waiting.

The bear case for waiting is unchanged. Amazon's trailing free cash flow is still negative $7.6 billion. Alphabet printed negative $5.9 billion in the second quarter. Meta printed $780 million. Oracle printed negative $23.7 billion for the fiscal year that ended in May. In July, three of the four large spenders raised the 2026 capex envelope and none cut it. The loop is paying for silicon out of the balance sheet and the bond market. It is not paying for silicon out of incremental AI cash.

## The price of a token

The July split in list prices is still the live fact. OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna by eighty percent on 30 July, to twenty cents of input and $1.20 of output per million tokens. Terra was cut twenty percent, to $2 and $12. Those cards are still on the official page this weekend. The new flagship, GPT-6 Astra, lists at $10 and $50. Anthropic's official cards put Claude Opus 5 at $5 and $25 and Sonnet 5 at $2 and $10. Google lists Gemini 3.1 Pro at $2 and $12. xAI lists Grok 4.6 at $2 and $6 below the long-context breakpoint.

Read that stack the way a lender would. The expensive models are still expensive. The cheap models got cheaper after the last generation of circular paper was already in motion. Volume can rescue the dollar total if people use enough of the cheap tokens. Nobody in a primary filing has shown that volume yet. Until they do, a lower list price is a lower conversion rate on the installed base.

## The price of an hour

GPU rental tells the same story by venue. The last published CCIR print, from Friday morning, put a guaranteed H100 at $10.53 an hour on a hyperscaler, $3.71 at a neocloud, and $3.03 on the marketplace. H200 printed $10.30, $4.40, and $4.00. B200 printed $14.24, $6.69, and $5.99. An interruptible H100 at a neocloud printed $2.15.

A residual-value guarantee written against a 2025 rental deck is looking at a different number than the deck. Hyperscaler stickers staying high does not rescue a neocloud that financed at the low print. The first place this race becomes cash is not NVIDIA's mark. It is a payment date at the rim.

## The paper

NVIDIA's August 8-K still caps residual-value guarantees at SB Energy's Ohio site at $105 billion, with OpenAI as the tenant. Amazon has closed $50 billion of OpenAI equity and added $100 billion of AWS commercial commitment over eight years. That paper did not shrink this week. It also did not have to be funded as a guarantee. Announced is not funded is not drawn. Drawn is the print that moves this letter from 3 to 5.

CoreWeave still carried about $35 billion of debt at the end of June. The July loan cleared wider than advertised. Financing still closed. A bounce in the equity is not the same thing as a cheaper refinancing.

## What would change the letter

The score moves to 4 if a named guarantee is restated, if a neocloud misses a payment, if another large cheap-tier cut arrives without a volume offset, or if NVIDIA falls more than fifteen percent from the May high while capex guides stay up.

The score moves to 5 if a residual-value guarantee is drawn, if a hyperscaler cuts capex, or if a hub credit event prints.

The score moves to 2 only if mid-tier token prices stop falling for two quarters, neocloud rental holds, and the circular book stops growing.

Next forced date on the calendar is Oracle's fiscal 2027 first quarter, due around 10 September. That print can change spender cash language. It cannot, by itself, unwrite the July token cut or the August guarantee.

## The open question

The loop is a race between a falling conversion rate and a rising stock of promises. Friday improved the collateral. It did not slow the race. The question worth keeping open is not whether the models work. The question is whether tokens sold, at these prices, can service paper that was sized on a higher price.

This letter does not tell the cash account what to own.
